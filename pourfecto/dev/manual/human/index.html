Preparing a Stock by Hand

The "human" configuration plans a protocol in which a person weighs powders and measures liquids. Pourfecto decides the amounts and the transfers. It does not plan mixing, filtering or labelling. A program that needs those steps takes the planned steps as data and adds its own.

Powders and liquids

A source that holds only solids, a Mixture, is a powder. A powder is weighed, and the planner counts the amount drawn from it in milligrams. Every other source is measured by volume and counted in microliters. amount_unit gives the unit of a source. Both units are on the same numeric scale, so min_vol_threshold applies to a weighed amount in mg as it does to a volume in µL.

A powder adds no volume to its target. The capacity of a target well limits only the liquid that is added, and the planned amount of the backfilling liquid, usually water, is therefore slightly more than needed for a concentrated stock. The person brings the stock to its final volume.

The "human" configuration

The head has two pistons that share one channel: a volume piston that measures from 1 mL to 10 L, and a mass piston that weighs from 1 mg to 10 kg. A powder source can only be drawn by a mass piston and a liquid source only by a volume piston (piston_dimension). If no configuration can draw a source, pourfecto throws a StockCompatibilityError that names the well, before it solves anything. A pipette configuration alone therefore rejects a powder, and in a run with "human" and "p200" the powder is always weighed by the human while the liquids use either.

The limits apply when enforce_minimum_shot=true. A target that needs less than the smallest amount is planned with the smallest amount, and solution_quality flags the difference. Volumes below 1 mL are better pipetted: include a pipette configuration in the same run.

A lab with different equipment builds its own configuration and registers it:

register_instrument!(human_configuration(min_mass=10u"mg", max_mass=2u"kg"); name="human_small_balance")

The plan as data

protocol_steps returns the plan as a DataFrame, with one row for each transfer: its kind ("weigh" or "pipette"), the component, the amount and its unit, the source labware and well, the target labware and well, and the instrument.

pc = pourfecto(sources, targets, ["human"])
steps = protocol_steps(pc)

The rows are ordered by target well, then weighing before pipetting, then source well. The order is the same for the same plan. Compiling a Pourcast writes protocol_steps.csv into the protocol folder of the Human configuration, with the sources in the order of the protocol's slotting.

Adjusting pH

Acid or base added to reach a pH is a planned amount like any other. Compute it with CHESSCore.adjust_pH, which returns the stock with the titrant added, and use the result as the target. The titrant is then a component of the target, and the plan draws it from a source of the titrant, liquid or solid.

Re-planning a half-made stock

Use allow_in_place=true and declare the target in full: what the target already holds plus what it still needs. The source labware is the half-made stock, and the target labware has the same name. The content already in the well stays, and only the missing amounts are planned.

A declaration that leaves out something already in the well has no feasible plan, and pourfecto throws an InfeasibleSolveError. A declaration that asks for less of a component than the well holds produces a plan that misses the target by more than the solution tolerance: solution_quality flags it, and pourfecto(directory, ...) refuses to compile it.

Dead volume

The "human" configuration adds no dead volume. dead_volume_buffer belongs to the Nimbus. A container's residual volume is the business of the program that supplies the sources, which subtracts it before planning.
