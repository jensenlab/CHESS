Reagents & Species

The material in a Well is described on two levels. A Reagent is the physical material that is weighed or pipetted: table salt, a bottle of ethanol. A Species is an entity in solution: Na⁺, Cl⁻, glucose. Table salt is one reagent, and once dissolved it is two species. A Dissolution connects the two by listing the species a reagent breaks into.

Each species carries an elemental formula such as C6H12O6. Atomic masses from the periodic table turn a formula into a molecular weight, and a reagent's formula follows from its species, so a stock's amount of any element can be counted. This chapter covers the formulas, the two types, and the dissolution that links them. Composition & Solution Chemistry covers what CHESS computes from them: molar amounts, element amounts, and pH.

Elemental formulas

A formula is written as a string. Element symbols are one capital letter with an optional lowercase letter, followed by an optional whole-number count, so CO is carbon monoxide and Co is cobalt. Parentheses or square brackets group atoms, and a middle dot or a period joins the parts of a hydrate, each with an optional whole-number coefficient. Elements may appear in any order, and a repeated element is summed. Three functions work with formulas. CHESSCore.parse_formula reads a string into a dictionary from element symbol to atom count, CHESSCore.formula_mass gives the molar mass from the atomic masses of the PeriodicTable.jl package, and CHESSCore.formula_string writes the counts back as a string in Hill order (carbon first, hydrogen second, then the other elements alphabetically):

julia> counts = CHESSCore.parse_formula("FeSO4·7H2O");

julia> sort(collect(counts))
4-element Vector{Pair{Symbol, Int64}}:
 :Fe => 1
  :H => 14
  :O => 11
  :S => 1

julia> round(u"g/mol", CHESSCore.formula_mass(counts); digits=3)
278.006 g mol⁻¹

julia> CHESSCore.formula_string(counts)
"FeH14O11S"

Charge is not part of a formula string. A species takes its charge as a separate argument, and a formula that contains a charge, an unknown element symbol, or an isotope is rejected:

julia> CHESSCore.parse_formula("Na+")
ERROR: ArgumentError: Invalid formula "Na+" at position 3: charge is not part of a formula; give it as a separate argument

Isotopes are not supported. Every formula describes the natural isotopic mixture, and the notation [13C] and the symbols D and T are reserved for a later extension, so they raise an error instead of being read as something else. A formula also cannot describe a fractional amount, such as the half water of a hemihydrate.

Species

A species has a name, an integer charge (zero for a neutral species), and a description of its atoms. The description is one of three kinds:

  • an elemental formula, from which the molecular weight is derived;
  • a stored molecular weight, for a species whose formula has not been registered yet; or
  • the marker opaque, for a species whose atoms are intentionally not described (see Opaque reagents).

CHESS registers common ions such as Na⁺, Cl⁻, and Ca²⁺. @species registers a new species from a label, a name, a charge, and a formula:

julia> @species Li⁺ "Li+" 1 "Li"
Li⁺

julia> round(u"g/mol", molecular_weight(Li⁺); digits=3)
6.94 g mol⁻¹

@spc_str recalls a registered species by name. The ASCII form spc"Na+" finds Na⁺.

A species is identified by its name. Two species are equal when their name, charge, and formula are equal, whatever order the elements were written in. Registering the same name and charge with a different formula therefore gives a different species and never a silent merge. Isomers are separate species with separate names, because a formula is a property of a species and not its identity:

julia> Species("D-glucose", 0, "C6H12O6") == Species("D-glucose", 0, "H12C6O6")
true

julia> Species("D-glucose", 0, "C6H12O6") == Species("D-fructose", 0, "C6H12O6")
false

A species does not store its structure, so stereoisomers such as D-glucose and L-glucose differ only by name. molecular_weight of a species with a formula comes from atomic masses, and the species has no stored weight that could disagree with it.

Reagent types

Reagent is an abstract type with three concrete subtypes, Solid, Liquid, and Gas, which share four fields: name, molecular_weight, density, and pubchemid. A solid is dosed by mass or amount and a liquid by volume. CHESS already includes a starter set of reagents, which @rgt_str recalls by name:

julia> rgt"water"
water

julia> molecular_weight(rgt"water")
18.015 g mol⁻¹

julia> density(rgt"water")
1.0 g mL⁻¹

julia> pubchemid(rgt"water")
962

Defining a reagent

@reagent registers a new reagent from a label, a name, a type, a description of its chemistry, a density, and a PubChem ID. The chemistry argument says what the reagent is made of. An elemental formula string registers the reagent as a single neutral species with that formula, and the molecular weight is derived from the formula:

julia> @reagent mysugar "my sugar" Solid "C6H12O6" 1.2u"g/mL" missing
my sugar

julia> molecular_weight(mysugar)
180.156 g mol⁻¹

julia> sort(collect(CHESSCore.formula(only(keys(dissolution(mysugar).products)))))
3-element Vector{Pair{Symbol, Int64}}:
 :C => 6
 :H => 12
 :O => 6

A Dissolution in the chemistry argument registers a reagent that breaks into several species (see Dissolution). A molar mass, such as 180.16u"g/mol", may be given in place of a formula for a reagent whose formula has not been registered. The reagent then keeps that stored molecular weight and has no formula. Any of the properties can be left unknown, so a reagent can be registered and used with incomplete data:

julia> @reagent myreagent "my made-up reagent" Solid missing missing missing
my made-up reagent

julia> molecular_weight(myreagent)
missing

An invalid formula string is rejected when the reagent is defined.

Opaque reagents

Some materials have no formula: an undefined mixture such as yeast extract, a polymer of varying length, or a product sold as a mixture of related compounds. The word opaque in the chemistry argument marks a reagent whose atoms are intentionally not described. Declaring it opaque is different from leaving the formula out, because atoms warns about a reagent with no registered formula and says nothing about an opaque one. An opaque reagent has no molecular weight unless one is given by calling opaque with a molar mass. That form suits a mixture sold as a salt, or a solution whose stated purity makes its effective molecular weight differ from the compound's:

julia> @reagent mymixture "my undefined mixture" Solid opaque 1.0u"g/mL" missing
my undefined mixture

julia> molecular_weight(mymixture)
missing

julia> @reagent mysolution "my 88% solution" Solid opaque(102.4u"g/mol") 1.2u"g/mL" missing
my 88% solution

julia> molecular_weight(mysolution)
102.4 g mol⁻¹

An opaque reagent with a molar mass has its moles tracked, so concentrations work, but its atoms are not counted by atoms.

Dissolution

Every Reagent has a dissolution, a Dissolution that lists the species it breaks down into when dissolved, with a coefficient for each. The default dissolution is the reagent's own identity as a single species, which means no dissociation:

julia> dissolution(rgt"water")
Dissolution(Dict{Species, Int64}(water => 1))

+ and * on species build a Dissolution. Adding species lists them, and multiplying by a whole number sets a coefficient, as for the two chlorides that balance Ca²⁺:

julia> salt = spc"Ca2+" + 2*spc"Cl-";

julia> salt.products[spc"Cl-"], salt.products[spc"Ca2+"]
(2, 1)

A dissolution in the chemistry argument of @reagent registers the reagent and its dissolution in one step. The molecular weight is derived from the species and their coefficients, so the two cannot disagree. A hydrate lists its water of hydration as a species:

julia> @reagent LiCl "lithium chloride" Solid (Li⁺ + spc"Cl-") missing missing
lithium chloride

julia> round(u"g/mol", molecular_weight(LiCl); digits=2)
42.39 g mol⁻¹

set_dissolution! registers the dissolution of a reagent that is already defined. The dissolutions are held in dissolutions, keyed by the reagent itself and not by its type, because every reagent is a value of one of three types and the type cannot tell two solids apart. A reagent is neutral, so the charges of its products, weighted by their coefficients, must sum to zero:

julia> @reagent DemoSalt "demo salt" Solid missing missing missing;

julia> @species DemoCation "DemoCation" 1 "K";

julia> @species DemoAnion "DemoAnion" -1 "Br";

julia> set_dissolution!(DemoSalt, DemoCation + DemoAnion);

julia> sort(string.(keys(dissolution(DemoSalt).products)))
2-element Vector{String}:
 "DemoAnion"
 "DemoCation"

julia> DemoSalt in keys(dissolutions)
true

julia> set_dissolution!(DemoSalt, Dissolution(Dict(DemoCation => 1)))
ERROR: ArgumentError: The dissolution of "demo salt" has a net charge of 1; a reagent is neutral, so the charges of its products must sum to zero

Dissolution coefficients must be non-negative. A base's hydroxide is represented by the canonical OH⁻ species, not a negative H⁺ count. This lets pH (see Composition & Solution Chemistry) net acid and base contributions by explicit subtraction instead of signed stoichiometry.

A weak acid or base only partially dissociates, so a fixed Dissolution cannot describe it. It is registered as an AcidBaseSystem instead. See Acid/Base Chemistry.
