Stocks

A Stock is a combination of organisms and chemicals. It is what a Well holds. Stock is an abstract type, and the concrete subtype depends on the contents:

  • Empty: nothing.
  • Mixture: solids only.
  • Solution: at least one liquid, and any solids.
  • Culture: at least one organism, and any solids and liquids. A culture can have no liquid, because an organism's Biomass is an absolute quantity, not one derived from the stock's volume. See Organisms & Cultures.

The generic Stock(organisms,solids,liquids) constructor picks the subtype by checking for organisms, then liquids, then solids:

OrganismsLiquidsSolidsResult
≥ 1anyanyCulture
0≥ 1anySolution
00≥ 1Mixture
000Empty
julia> Empty()
Empty Stock

Building a stock from a quantity

Multiplying a quantity by a Reagent builds a stock:

julia> water_solution = 1u"mL" * rgt"water"
1.00 mL Solution (1 reagent(s))
 Liquids  Name   Amount   Concentration
────────────────────────────────────────
 water    water  1.00 mL          100 %

julia> salt = 5u"g" * rgt"sodium_chloride"
5.00 g Mixture (1 reagent(s))
 Solids           Name             Amount  Concentration
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 sodium_chloride  Sodium Chloride  5.00 g          100 %

Mixing stocks

Adding two stocks produces the subtype that the combined contents call for. Water plus salt is a Solution:

julia> saline = water_solution + salt
1.00 mL Solution (2 reagent(s))
 Solids           Name             Amount  Concentration
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 sodium_chloride  Sodium Chloride  5.00 g    5.00 g mL⁻¹

 Liquids  Name   Amount   Concentration
────────────────────────────────────────
 water    water  1.00 mL          100 %

quantity(::Stock) reports the total liquid volume only (1.00 mL here, excluding the dissolved solid). Solids contribute to volume_estimate, which estimates from density and warns when a solid's density is unknown. CHESS's sodium_chloride is registered without a density:

julia> volume_estimate(salt)
┌ Warning: volume_estimate: density unknown for Sodium Chloride; excluded from the estimate (result is a lower bound)
└ @ CHESSCore ...
0 mL

Scaling

Multiplying or dividing a stock by a plain number scales every reagent and every organism's Biomass proportionally. Multiplying by a quantity scales the whole stock so that the quantity is its new total. This is how diluting a Culture to a target volume also dilutes its organisms:

julia> double = 2*saline
2.00 mL Solution (2 reagent(s))
 Solids           Name             Amount  Concentration
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 sodium_chloride  Sodium Chloride  10.0 g    5.00 g mL⁻¹

 Liquids  Name   Amount   Concentration
────────────────────────────────────────
 water    water  2.00 mL          100 %

julia> tenmL = 10u"mL" * saline
10.0 mL Solution (2 reagent(s))
 Solids           Name             Amount  Concentration
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 sodium_chloride  Sodium Chloride  50.0 g    5.00 g mL⁻¹

 Liquids  Name   Amount   Concentration
────────────────────────────────────────
 water    water  10.0 mL          100 %

The non-negativity constraint

Subtracting one stock from another throws MixingError if any reagent would go negative. The same applies to organism Biomass, which subtraction can reduce or remove like a chemical amount (see Removing organisms):

julia> saline - double
ERROR: Mixing Error with sodium_chloride
: attempted to add a negative quantity to a Stock

Components

Reagents and organisms are the two kinds of component that a stock tracks. Both are StockComponents. all_reagents lists the solid and liquid reagents of a stock or of a vector of stocks without duplicates, and all_components also lists the organisms. set_component returns a copy of a stock with one component at an exact quantity, which is a mass for a solid, a volume for a liquid, and a Biomass for an organism. A zero quantity removes the component:

julia> all_reagents(saline)
2-element Vector{Reagent}:
 sodium_chloride
 water

julia> all_components(saline + 1u"OD*mL" * org"SMU_UA159")
3-element Vector{StockComponent}:
 sodium_chloride
 water
 SMU_UA159

julia> set_component(saline, rgt"sodium_chloride", 2u"g")
1.00 mL Solution (2 reagent(s))
 Solids           Name             Amount  Concentration
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 sodium_chloride  Sodium Chloride  2.00 g    2.00 g mL⁻¹

 Liquids  Name   Amount   Concentration
────────────────────────────────────────
 water    water  1.00 mL          100 %

julia> set_component(saline, rgt"sodium_chloride", 0u"g")
1.00 mL Solution (1 reagent(s))
 Liquids  Name   Amount   Concentration
────────────────────────────────────────
 water    water  1.00 mL          100 %

reagent_df tabulates a vector of stocks with one row for each stock and one column for each reagent. The cells hold the concentration of the reagent, or its quantity with measure=quantity. component_df also has columns for organisms. The column names come from component_to_string. It uses the registered name of a component when it finds the component in the reagent_context that is passed as a keyword, and the display name otherwise:

julia> reagent_df([saline, tenmL])
2×2 DataFrame
 Row │ Sodium Chloride  water
     │ Any              Any
─────┼──────────────────────────
   1 │ 5.0 g mL⁻¹       100.0 %
   2 │ 5.0 g mL⁻¹       100.0 %

julia> reagent_df([saline, tenmL]; measure=quantity)
2×2 DataFrame
 Row │ Sodium Chloride  water
     │ Any              Any
─────┼─────────────────────────────
   1 │ 5 g              1000 μL
   2 │ 50.0 g           10000.0 μL

reagent_display returns the solids, liquids, and organisms of a stock as dictionaries from name to amount and concentration, which are the values that the printed table of a stock shows:

julia> solids, liquids, organisms = reagent_display(saline);

julia> collect(keys(solids))
1-element Vector{String}:
 "Sodium Chloride"

julia> solids["Sodium Chloride"]["Amount"]
(5.0, "g")

Named stocks

@stock registers a named stock, like @location_kind, @reagent, @chemical, and @organism. @stock_str recalls it. Only stocks registered this way can be recalled, so an intermediate stock, such as a concentrated solution combined into a larger recipe, is never recalled by accident:

julia> @stock saline_recipe 1u"mL" * rgt"water" + 5u"g" * rgt"sodium_chloride"
1.00 mL Solution (2 reagent(s))
 Solids           Name             Amount  Concentration
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 sodium_chloride  Sodium Chloride  5.00 g    5.00 g mL⁻¹

 Liquids  Name   Amount   Concentration
────────────────────────────────────────
 water    water  1.00 mL          100 %

saline_recipe is also bound as a constant where it was registered. stock"..." recalls stocks registered by CHESS or a lab module, such as CHESS's LB broth recipe:

julia> stock"lb_1000mL"
1.00 L Solution (2 reagent(s))
 Solids  Name      Amount  Concentration
─────────────────────────────────────────
 lb      LB Broth  25.0 g   25.0 mg mL⁻¹

 Liquids  Name   Amount  Concentration
───────────────────────────────────────
 water    water  1.00 L          100 %
